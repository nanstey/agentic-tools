# Research: Anthropic code review practices

## Summary
Anthropic's managed Code Review uses parallel issue-finding agents, verification against source, deduplication and severity ranking; its public check never itself blocks or approves a merge. The Claude Code team's blog describes a stricter internal process—independent agent voting, mandatory thread resolution, risk-tiered human review and convergence controls—which is evidence of internal practice, not a guarantee of public-service behavior. Adopt these mechanisms selectively with measured quality and cost guardrails, not as justification for unqualified zero-human merging.

### Historical product changes and numerical claims
- [Launch announcement](https://claude.com/resources/articles/code-review), “How it works” / “Cost and control,” described research preview, automatic new-PR reviews, an approximately 20-minute average and generally $15–25 average review costs. Current docs add trigger modes, depth levels and detailed usage-credit budgeting; the blog says the product is now generally available on Team/Enterprise. Treat old averages as historical observations, not current quotes/SLA.
- Launch “Code Review in action” reports 84% of >1,000-line PRs had findings (7.5 issues average), versus 31% of <50-line PRs (0.5 issues), and “less than 1% of findings are marked incorrect.” These are explicitly sourced launch observations, **not** risk thresholds or a measured universal false-positive rate: reporting/labeling and sampling limitations are unspecified.
- Launch and blog report substantive-comment coverage rising 16%→54%. That measures coverage, not correctness or incident prevention.
- The blog describes stricter internal merge policy while expressly acknowledging that the public product “posts findings, not approvals.” Do not conflate their evolving internal zero-human paths with the service contract.

## Proposed adaptations — ours, not Anthropic requirements
1. **Adopt:** separate discovery from independent verification; require source-backed failure mechanism and affected `file:line` before posting. Keep verifier input independent where feasible; no invented voting quorum or confidence threshold. Evidence: findings 1–2, 6.
2. **Adopt:** parallelize genuinely different review angles, and increase depth for blast radius/complexity rather than line count alone. Reject fixed agent counts inferred from launch examples. Evidence: 2, 9, 12.
3. **Adopt:** short project context plus short review-only policy, trusted from an approved base revision. Explicitly test policy compliance; keep policy changes separate from the change being judged. Evidence: 6–7 and blog §1.
4. **Adopt:** clear correctness-focused labels, pre-existing attribution, deduplication and explicit evidence requirements. Tune nits/exclusions to repository failure modes; no universal five-nit cap—the docs' five is an example. Evidence: 1, 3, 6.
5. **Adopt:** bounded fix/review loops with escalation, later diff-focused passes and no repeated findings; retain explicit full-review escape hatch. Three rounds is an internal precedent, not our settled limit. Evidence: 10.
6. **Adopt:** independently required CI/security checks and trusted human approval for sensitive paths; scope approvals to reviewed commits. **Reject:** a bot replacing a required person, or no-findings being treated as proof of safety. Evidence: 4, 8–9.
7. **Adopt:** deliberate trigger/cost policy and representative pilot with a spend cap; distinguish one-shot from subscribed review and handle draft/fork exceptions. Treat skipped/missing verdicts as unknown. **Reject:** default every-push rollout without cost/convergence evidence. Evidence: 4–5, 12.
8. **Adopt:** incident/revert regression evals, clean-change noise tests and feedback categories; measure review rounds and human wait alongside quality. **Reject:** “<1% marked incorrect” as our false-positive target or evidence that zero-human merges are safe. Evidence: 11 and historical claims.

## Gaps
- No current published agent counts, dynamic allocation algorithm, verifier quorum, numerical confidence threshold, independent-verifier correlation analysis or universal false-positive rate.
- No causal experiment establishing that parallel review alone caused revert improvements; blog measurements are internal and lack full evaluation datasets/methods.
- No documented public-service hard round cap, automatic risk classifier approval policy, or complete security-review execution boundary in these pages. Internal security-review practices must not be attributed to the managed bug-review service.
- Exact publication dates, raw HTTP status/redirect chains and canonical URLs were not exposed by the readable fetch output. Requested pages were nevertheless available and read in full.
- Next planning gate: choose our risk owners, allowed reviewer authority, evidence schema, escalation condition and pilot metrics. Any numeric thresholds require an explicit local decision and baseline—not extrapolation from these sources.
